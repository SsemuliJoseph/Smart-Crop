    Disease Library

    Detailed information on all detectable potato diseases

    🌿

    Healthy

    Normal plant — no disease detected

    ✅ No Action

    Description

    A healthy potato plant shows vibrant green leaves with no lesions, spots, wilting, or discolouration. Proper crop management maintains plant health throughout the growing season.

    What to look for

    • Deep green leaves with no spots
    • Firm, upright stems
    • Even growth across the field
    • No unusual odours from the plant

    Maintain Health

    • Apply balanced NPK fertiliser at planting
    • Ensure adequate spacing (30–40cm between plants)
    • Water regularly — avoid waterlogging
    • Monitor weekly for early disease signs
    • Remove weeds that compete for nutrients
    🍂

    Late Blight

    Phytophthora infestans

    🔴 Critical

    Description

    Late Blight is the most destructive potato disease in the world — responsible for the Irish Potato Famine. It can destroy an entire field within days if untreated.

    Symptoms

    • Water-soaked, dark green lesions on leaf edges
    • White fluffy mould under leaves in humid conditions
    • Lesions turn brown and the leaf dies rapidly
    • Brown rot extending into tubers
    • A distinct unpleasant smell from infected plants

    Causes & Spread

    • Caused by Phytophthora infestans (water mould)
    • Spreads via airborne spores in cool (10–25°C) wet weather
    • Can travel several kilometres in wind
    • Infected seed tubers are a major source

    🚨 Immediate Treatment

    • Apply Mancozeb 80% WP at 2.5kg/ha immediately
    • Or use Chlorothalonil 75% WP at 2kg/ha
    • Remove and destroy all visibly infected leaves/plants
    • Improve air circulation — avoid overhead irrigation
    • Re-apply fungicide every 7–10 days while wet weather continues
    • Do NOT compost infected material
    🟤

    Early Blight

    Alternaria solani

    🟠 Moderate

    Description

    Early Blight appears on older, lower leaves and progresses upward. Less devastating than Late Blight but still causes significant yield loss if left untreated, especially in stressed plants.

    Symptoms

    • Dark brown spots with distinctive concentric rings (target pattern)
    • Lesions surrounded by yellow halo
    • Starts on lowest, oldest leaves and moves upward
    • Leaves turn yellow then brown and die
    • Leathery dark lesions may appear on tubers

    Causes & Spread

    • Caused by the fungus Alternaria solani
    • Spreads via wind-blown spores and rain splash
    • Favoured by warm (24–29°C), humid conditions with dry periods
    • Weakened plants (nutrient-stressed) are most susceptible

    🟠 Treatment

    • Apply Copper Oxychloride 50% WP at 2kg/ha
    • Or use Mancozeb + Metalaxyl combination
    • Remove infected lower leaves before spraying
    • Ensure adequate nitrogen fertilisation to reduce stress
    • Spray every 10–14 days during humid periods
    • Rotate crops — avoid planting potatoes in same field for 2+ years
    🥀

    Bacterial Wilt

    Ralstonia solanacearum

    🟣 Severe

    Description

    Bacterial Wilt is a soil-borne disease with no chemical cure. Once it infects a field, the bacteria can persist in soil for years. Prevention and crop rotation are the only effective strategies.

    Symptoms

    • Sudden wilting of shoots even when soil has adequate moisture
    • Wilting starts in youngest leaves and progresses down
    • Brown discolouration of stem vascular tissue when cut
    • Milky bacterial ooze from cut stems in water
    • Rotting and browning inside tubers

    Causes & Spread

    • Caused by Ralstonia solanacearum (Race 3, Biovar 2)
    • Spreads through infected soil, water, tools, and seed tubers
    • Can survive in soil for up to 6 years
    • Waterlogged, warm soils favour the bacteria

    🟣 Management (No Chemical Cure)

    • Remove and destroy all infected plants immediately — do NOT compost
    • Do not replant potatoes in the same soil for at least 2 seasons
    • Use certified disease-free seed tubers only
    • Disinfect tools with bleach solution (1:9 ratio) between plants
    • Improve field drainage to reduce waterlogging
    • Rotate with non-host crops: maize, wheat, legumes

    PotatoGuard

    AI-Powered Irish Potato Disease Detection System

    v1.0.0 · 2025

    📖 Project Overview

    PotatoGuard was developed as a final year project for BSc Computer Science at Makerere University (2024/2025). It uses a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) trained on the PlantVillage dataset to detect Irish potato leaf diseases in real time.

    The system integrates AWS Lambda, SageMaker, DynamoDB, and S3 to provide a scalable, serverless backend capable of processing thousands of simultaneous detections.

    🎯 Mission

    Irish potatoes are a critical food and income crop for millions of Ugandan smallholder farmers. Late Blight alone can destroy an entire crop in less than two weeks. PotatoGuard puts expert-level disease diagnosis in every farmer's pocket — no internet connection required for the mobile app.
